Borrow the platform, keep the labs: picasso and Determinate Nix

picasso already stands on Determinate Systems' work: its workstation runs Determinate Nix, its README installs it, and every CI job starts with their installer Action. This study puts what Determinate Systems sells and gives away next to what picasso is, side by side, and asks one practical question: what can a one-person flake repository take from that platform, and add on top of it, between October 2026 and April 2027?

Matheus Hoffmann · Escola Politécnica, Universidade Federal do Rio de Janeiro · version 1.0, 12 October 2026

Scope. Every claim about Determinate Systems comes from its public product pages, documentation, blog or GitHub repositories, fetched on 12 October 2026 and numbered in §08. Performance figures are the vendor's and were not measured here. Every claim about picasso names a file at commit 140dc91.
Drafted with the assistance of Claude Code (Anthropic) and reviewed by the author.

Summary

The relation. Determinate Systems and picasso are not on the same layer. Determinate ships a Nix distribution (a fork of NixOS/nix under LGPL-2.1 [2]), an installer, a flake registry with semantic versioning (FlakeHub), a paid binary cache and a paid, SLA-backed package set [1, 3, 6, 9]. picasso is six self-contained flakes, called labs, that pin toolchains for one person's work (wave modelling, PDFs, linting, agents, CUDA, presenting) and that other repositories borrow [16]. picasso is a customer of the first layer, not a competitor to it.

What we found. picasso depends on Determinate in four places (§03). Two of them are stale: the publisher Action pins nix-installer-action@v16 and magic-nix-cache-action@v9, a release from January 2025 that predates the cache's June 2025 revival [11, 12]. Five of the six lab locks are older than the 30 days that Determinate's free flake-checker flags [13].

Six months. Seven items, all free tiers or open-source tools, sized for one person's evenings (§05). The core is: fix the stale Action, add the free checks, publish the labs to FlakeHub as public flakes on each release tag, and keep picasso's own differentiators, which are domain toolchains, a bit-for-bit rebuild check and citable releases. Things picasso will not do are listed too: an SLA, FIPS, SOC 2, a private cache, a Nix fork.

01two different layers

Determinate Systems describes Determinate Nix as its "validated and secure downstream distribution of NixOS/nix designed for enterprise use cases" [2]. The product page sells it on speed (lazy trees, parallel evaluation), on operations (managed garbage collection, MDM roll-out, OpenTelemetry traces) and on compliance (SOC 2 Type II; the footer adds FedRAMP High) [1]. Around the distribution sit FlakeHub, a registry for flakes, and FlakeHub Cache and Determinate Secure Packages, which are paid [6, 8, 9].

picasso's AGENTS.md describes itself as "self-contained Nix flakes ('labs') for the toolchains I work in, on an Ubuntu workstation (i9 + RTX 4090) running Determinate Nix — not NixOS" [16]. Its unit is the lab: one directory with its own flake.nix, lock, justfile, README and lab.json, that references nothing outside itself, so that ww-lab can check out labs/pratico as a sparse submodule and marola can pin ?dir=labs/lint.

the honest frame

Determinate Systems is a company with a staff, a compliance programme and enterprise customers. picasso is one person's repository with CI on free GitHub-hosted runners, two downstream consumers (ww-lab, marola) and the profile README. Nothing in this study proposes that picasso match the vendor's scope. The useful question is narrower: which of the vendor's free parts make the labs better, and what do the labs give that a general platform does not.

05what picasso can do in six months

Each item follows the lab contract in picasso's AGENTS.md: a lab stays self-contained, every check builds in the sandbox, CI runs with --no-update-lock-file. Nothing here makes one lab depend on another, or on FlakeHub: a consumer that pins github:h0ffmann/picasso?dir=… keeps working.

D1 · fix the publisher Action (October–November 2026)

Bump nix-installer-action from v16 to the v23 CI already uses, and either move magic-nix-cache-action to a release after the June 2025 revival (v11 or later) or remove it. First, read the cache step's log in a recent ww-lab or h0ffmann/h0ffmann run to see whether v9 caches at all. Effort: one PR, plus the two consumers' next pin bump. Cost: none.

D2 · flake-checker in CI as a report (November 2026)

Add flake-checker-action to the labs matrix with its default exit status 0, so it writes a summary and fails nothing. Today it would flag five labs for age. It gives a visible number for a question the lab cards on the profile README already ask (the lock date). Effort: a few lines in ci.yml. Cost: none.

D3 · publish each lab to FlakeHub on a release tag (December 2026–January 2027)

A job in release.yml with a six-row matrix that runs flakehub-push with directory: labs/<lab>, tag: v…, visibility: public. Consumers can then write https://flakehub.com/f/h0ffmann/<lab>/0.1 and get semver ranges, while the GitHub URL keeps working. Open questions: one repo-wide tag gives every lab the same version even when it did not change; the flake name must start with the GitHub org (h0ffmann/…); publishing needs id-token: write. The owner decides, since publishing is outward-facing and a published release stays. Effort: one PR and a dry run on an unlisted flake. Cost: none for public flakes.

D4 · a weekly lock bump with a cooldown (January 2027)

update-flake-lock on a weekly schedule, one PR per lab, so the existing CI gate decides whether a bump is safe. To copy the vendor's seven-day cooldown [17] without changing the input's owner (which flake-checker and the lab contract both expect to be NixOS), the job can pin nixos-unstable to the newest revision that is at least seven days old instead of the tip. Switching the labs to DeterminateSystems/nixpkgs-weekly is the other option; it gets the cooldown free but makes every lab depend on FlakeHub, which the owner should decide. Effort: small; the seven-day pick is a short script with a --self-test, per AGENTS.md. Cost: CI minutes only.

D5 · a best-effort SBOM per lab (February 2027)

Generate a CycloneDX SBOM of each lab's lib.tools closure on each release and attach it to the GitHub release, from open-source tools in the lab's own nixpkgs (for example sbomnix, if the pinned nixpkgs carries it; not checked yet). This is not Secure Packages: no signing beyond cache.nixos.org's, no VEX curation, no SLA. It is a list a consumer can scan. Effort: medium. Cost: none.

D6 · the determinate NixOS module in the root (when the machine moves)

AGENTS.md allows the root to be brought to the current NixOS release while the workstation moves back to NixOS. The vendor's determinate NixOS module writes nix.custom.conf from nix.settings [3], so setup-cuda-cache's job becomes a few lines of configuration on that machine. Depends on: the human, the hardware and nixos-generate-config; no agent runs nixos-rebuild. Cost: none.

D7 · measure, don't quote (March 2027)

Two small measurements that turn vendor claims into picasso's own numbers: evaluation time and disk use of nix flake check per lab with lazy trees on and off, and a CI job that rebuilds each lab's checks.* with nix build --rebuild and reports which outputs are not bit-identical, as the whitepaper's experiment B did for one image. Effort: medium. Cost: CI minutes; the rebuild job runs weekly, not on every push, to stay inside free runner time.

what picasso will not do
  • No SLA, no CVE deadlines, no FIPS, no SOC 2. They need a team that answers within 24 hours; one person cannot honestly promise that.
  • No private cache or private flakes. Both are paid on FlakeHub, and the labs are public.
  • No Nix fork and no patches to Nix. picasso uses the Nix it is given.
  • No dependency of a lab on FlakeHub unless the owner chooses it in D4: the lab contract says a lab references nothing outside itself, and a GitHub URL is the floor every consumer can rely on.

06risks and limits

  • Vendor dependence. picasso's install path, CI and publisher Action all go through one company's tooling. The installer already calls upstream Nix unsupported [7]. The mitigation is cheap: the labs are plain flakes and still evaluate with upstream Nix, and D3 adds FlakeHub as a second address, not a replacement.
  • Vendor numbers. "3x", "20x" and "50%" in Table 1 are the vendor's claims for some flakes. They were not measured on picasso; D7 is how to find out.
  • Prices. No public price list was found for the paid tiers, so no paid option is costed here.
  • Bus factor of one. Every item is sized so that it can stop half done without breaking a consumer.
  • Snapshot. Determinate ships often (3.5 to 3.8 in two months of 2025 [4]); read this study as of 12 October 2026.

07conclusion

Determinate Systems builds the road; picasso is a small workshop next to it. The workshop already uses the road, and two of its signs are out of date. In six months, without paying for anything, picasso can fix those signs, add the free lock checks, appear on FlakeHub under semver, copy the idea of a cooldown, and produce two things a general platform does not: domain toolchains that build in CI, and its own measured rebuild results. Those are the parts worth the owner's evenings.
